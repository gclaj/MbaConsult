# Chicago Booth — Application Playbook

Booth
Start here! Read this advice to learn what makes someone "Boothy" (a.k.a. a good fit for Booth)


Booth's unique culture is one where you're allowed to do what you want (well, in terms of classes you choose) and it prides itself on students (politely) challenging each others' assumptions in order to drive the learning forward.
Because of that, these are the traits that show that someone is "Boothy" (you can just look at the static list below, or watch the video to see how I arrived at these traits):
- Able to challenge / persuade others (in a respectful way)
- Ideally using data, frameworks, etc. (vs. emotion / "gut feels")
- Are able to handle ambiguous situations (both as individuals, and helping a group manage through uncharted waters), ideally using a "True North" value system to guide them
- Have demonstrated perserverance / resilience in overcoming obstacles
- Have broader, genuine intellectual curiosity about things beyond merely "what I need to know to be good at my job"
Given that, which types of stories often work well for Booth, be it in the main essays, interview-invite essays, interview, etc.? Here are some examples of the types of situations many business school applicants have been in:

"Career Vision / Why Our School?" because that's what this question is
Video discussion
(Note that in the past, this larger essay question was displayed side-by-side with the mini text boxes; that has changed now - you can find these questions at the bottom of the "Chicago Booth & You" section of the application --  but the questions themselves haven't, so this advice still applies. Why? Because the "Booth & You" section of the application comes first, so they will more than likely read the 250 character version first and THEN move on to the essay... just like they did in the past, but now it's simply in 2 different places vs on the same page):
Text overview
Booth actually asks 3 separate career questions:
What is your immediate post-MBA career goal? (250 characters max)
What is your long-term post-MBA career goal? (250 characters max)
These are in the "Booth & You" section of the application 
How will the Booth MBA help you achieve your immediate and long-term post-MBA career goals? Please use the box below or upload a word document. (250 word MINIMUM - not maximum!)
Because this is three separate questions (with three separate response areas), it unfortunately makes it more different to build a cohesive narrative arc and forces you to be a bit more clinical in your approach. 
My advice by question:
For the 2 super-short career questions, the number 1 thing here is to not repeat words / phrases from one to the other -- that is, don't simply copy-n-paste stuff from the little boxes into the essay since: lazy and therefore not a good look.
Again though -- since you get theoretically an UNLIMITED number of words for the "Why Booth?" essay (NOT that you should USE UP an unlimited number!)... that definitely lowers the pressure on these text boxes, since any / all of the below can easily go into the main essay. 
1. What is your immediate post-MBA career goal? (250 characters max)
- In order to save space, start with a verb to describe the move you're making (To transition to...; To apply my existing ___ skills; To expand my role...)
The "verb" statement helps adcoms "connect the dots" between your current role and future plans (so they don't have to flip back and forth between this, your resume and other materials) to understand how things fit together:
- Pick a function, industry, and maybe 2 employers
 You can look up some previous employers here -- this link should auto-forward to the latest list by year; if it's from a previous year, select a new year from the drop-down at the top: https://www.chicagobooth.edu/employmentreport/employer/
- If you have room, include a reason WHY this short-term job makes sense (given your LT goal) -- that is, what will this job give you that is important for your long-term goal?
- "...this will teach me how to connect with consumers; vital for launching my own product."
- "Investment banking will give me the foundation I'll need to reach my long-term goal: leading M&A for a major cloud company."
- If necessary, give at least ONE piece of evidence that you'll get the job (either existing experience or "homework"). It might be tough to find room here, so then mention it in the longer essay.
- "To apply the communication and problem-solving skills I've learned as a math teacher to work at either the cryptocurrency division of a large bank, or for a startup, such as NewCo. I'm inspired by my TfA mentor, who made a similar switch."  (237 characters)
- "Current MBB consultants have told me that my client-management skills will be useful for recruiting."
- "Current students from the Rustandy Center mentioned that over 20 students got internships in EdTech last year; I'm confident I can make the switch."
 2. What is your long-term post-MBA career goal? (250 characters max)
- Starting this box with a standard "hook" might be a little weird due to space and "flow." I'd recommend starting with a more matter-of-fact description of the impact or long-term vision...save the "hook" for the main essay... but you can certainly REFERENCE it here:
- "In order to impact the cryptocurrency market for low-income teenagers, to teach them financial management skills, my long-term goal is start a company that provides lessons and mock trading accounts for teens."  (209 characters) <-- a reference to the hook, without taking up the space to write the whole thing out
3. How will the Booth MBA help you achieve your immediate and long-term post-MBA career goals? Please use the box below or upload a word document. (250 word MINIMUM - not maximum!)
Anything you don't mention in #1 and #2 that would otherwise go in "one tidy essay" will go here. 
Things I think you MUST mention: 
- Flexible curriculum / you can have multiple concentrations - show how you plan to take advantage of that (and make sure that this ties in to either your short/long term goal.) 
- Pick at least ONE "long-term" elective that will help you in the future (eg, management, fundraising)
- Clubs: pick at least one professional club ("fun" clubs will go in the second essay), show how it ties into either your short/long term goal (and don't forget how to talk about how you will CONTRIBUTE to the club/other members)
If you have a powerful hook, you might want to figure out how to work it in here even though the "flow"might be a little choppy - it might be a little awkward, but I would choose good content over good flow. 
A note on length... while there is no technical MAXIMUM length here, don't forget that with questions that don't have stated maximums, you're being judged on your judgement! I'd advise no more than 500 words here, and you can probably cover everything you need to (if you do so succinctly) in 350 words. 

Some sample examples of how #1 and #2 "work together" (and also show just how short 250 characters is)... 
Note that Booth is always talking about things like how Booth "empowers you to make an impact on the world in a way that not only significantly matters to you, but also changes the lives of others" and how Booth creates "bold leaders who accept the risk and reward of seeing the world differently.. shatter set norms... and uncover groundbreaking solutions" so I've tried to incorporate those themes into these examples and would recommend that you do the best to do the same in your statements....
Example 1: 
Short-term (213 characters) - "To work in product management for a company working on the cutting-edge of regulatory compliance, such as Ascent. This would broaden and build upon my previous experience leading software teams at Bank of America." (emphasis added to show the clear link from past to future)
Long-term (245 characters) - "Digital technologies are transforming banking; companies need to be sure that they're innovating within legal frameworks. With Gartner estimating the 2025 market size of this industry as $3b, I want to start my own B2B compliance software firm."
This person might go on to talk about the following in the main essay:
- How they, personally, got interested in banking tech
- Booth's entrepreneurship / innovation offerings
- How "Big Data"coursework is key to future business 
- How they could help build out the fledgling FinTech club by inviting their current boss (a compliance expert) to speak
- How a business plan competition and Booth's culture of inquiry will ensure idea refinement
Example 2: 
Short term (244 characters) - "After 3 years working on PE hospitality investment, post-MBA, I'd want to continue in PE, but expanding my focus to include investments in Asia. In this role, I'd transition to sourcing and negotiating deals, as well as leading Analyst teams." (emphasis added to show the clear link from past to future)
Long term (249 characters) - "Chinese consumers are leading a global tourism boom: our portfolio properties have seen a 15% increase in spend from mainland guests alone. Long term, I want to lead PE investments in boutique hotels in the Asian region, to capitalize on this trend." 
This person might go on to talk about the following in their main essay:
- "Origin story": how did THIS candidate get interested in the hospitality industry in the first place? Why Asia? 
- Flexibility! How a combination of PE + Booth's Strategy Lab + an elective "Managing Service Operations" would give them a mix of experiences and add up to exactly what they want/need. 
- How they'd join and participate in two specific clubs, PE Club and Travel, Transportation + Hospitality Club
- How they'd contribute by mentoring PE types and lead a trek (based on a history of doing so/specific relevant skills)
At the end of the day, figuring out how to best "split" a traditional cohesive essay into the three distinct pieces will require a bit of trial and error based on your unique situation/story. Go with what works best!

What should I say my post-college job(s) will be? 
If possible!!! Make the next few years about non-businessy stuff -- only because well, you'll get to do all of the businessy stuff during / post MBA. And if you’re currently going into something like consulting, consider mentioning your *second* job post-college as being something “in industry” that ties into the hook. 
Examples:
If your future career is some combination of (technology + business), or (the arts + business), etc., then you can use the next few years getting a first-hand view of the non-business parts of the field. So if the long-term goal is to run an organic vineyard*, spend the next few years stomping grapes, vs. working in a vineyard's accounts receivable department. 
In sum, talk about how you'll use the next few years to learn the technical / specific ins-and-outs of your field... and then you'll be using the MBA to transition to "management" later. 
If you're going to a start-up after college: since acceptance to Booth now allows you to take career risks now -- if you've currently got an entrepreneurial venture you've started, saying that you want to spend a few years learning about building a business first-hand prior to b-school will be perfect, since then you'll arrive at b-school with a more solid idea of what you want your future business to be (and it won't be the end of the world if your current business fails, since you've got an acceptance to Booth guaranteed)
If you ARE currently going into a business-type role after college, don't panic! While HBS started its 2 + 2 program explicitly to go after non-business types, my guess is that Booth did it to try to get HBS types early. In words of Queen Bey: they're gonna "put a ring on it" before you apply to HBS / Wharton / Etc. in 5 years anyway -- by making you commit to them, they are taking a chance on you, but also hoping to draw stronger talent to them! 
* This is another way of saying that if you're going into consulting / banking post-college and do NOT get in to a deferred program, it is probably less about you and more about "Well, we will see you in the applicant pool in about 5 years anyway"!
Anyway, for businessy types, it's FINE to talk about the foundational skills you hope to learn in the next few years (After all, a TON of people go into MBA programs from things like consulting / banking!) -- however, my advice is to say that after your Analyst program in consulting / banking is done, that you plan to spend 1 - 2 years in a logical role in the "Hook Field". 
So if we keep the vineyard example* from above, here you could say you'll work for 2 years at Bain, where you'll learn [strategic frameworks or whatever] and then you'll try to get a mid-level managerial job at a wine company, so that way you'll arrive to Booth in a few years with a deeper appreciation of / knowledge to succeed in [Future Hook Industry].  Put another way, the 2nd half of your pre-MBA path / essay should look an awful lot like what the non-businessy types are going to say!

[The advice for the optional essay is basically the same for all schools, so if you've read this for another school, it will be the same here!]

Keep all optional essays:

Brief

Factual

Not whiny ("poor me / boo hoo!")

Not too apologetic ("I totally suck! You'll probably reject me!")

YES: MATURE. If you made a mistake, own up to it and what you learned from it. :)
If possible, put a “silver lining" spin on things -- yes, maybe that 20-hr-per-week job meant that your grades were low, but on the plus side, it taught you how to manage others, so net-net, it was a good thing for you!
For not using your boss as your recommender: Just quickly explain why, and ALSO why this other person is also qualified to speak about you.

"My current boss does not support my plans for business school, so instead I'm submitting a recommendation from another VP in my department, with whom I have worked very closely for the past two years. She has seen me organize projects, work with others, and sell senior management on new ideas, so I feel that she can provide a strong perspective on my leadership style."

For poor grades:There are all sorts of ways to phrase it, but just state the reason for the bad grades in a matter-of-fact way, followed by the other evidence (of either academic ability, work-ethic, or both) that you have. See if you can stick in (SUBTLY!) a positive personality trait / or "good lesson learned" in there too, such as not giving up:
First of all, if you did have a major life-changing event that led to poor academics / periods of unemployment, consider weaving it into a main essay story, to address it head-on.
Otherwise, state the reasons why the bad grades occurred (don’t try to gain sympathy; they’ve seen it all) and then provide “Evidence" that you’re going to be just fine in their program (e.g. extra coursework; a quantitative job). Reassure them that you are more than ready to thrive in their rigorous classroom environment.

"Coming from an under-funded public high school with no AP science/math classes (very few science classes, period), adapting to the engineering curriculum at Cal was a bumpy ride, to say the least. While it was tempting to drop out and major in economics instead, I persevered and ultimately completed the BSE degree. I'm glad I stuck with it, since it taught me that I could tackle any challenge. I'd respectfully ask that the Admissions Committee look at my GMAT score and my strong dedication to my job as proof that I will be able to thrive in the MBA academic environment. "

For low GMAT score (especially quant): If you had a bad day / something traumatic happen prior to the test, or if you simply don’t test well, then point to other “evidence" that you can compete at the same level of your future peers Please don’t say: “I don’t test well!" Guess what? Business school is full of exams! So that excuse will make them wonder if you’ll thrive there. Instead :

"I do not believe that my GMAT score represents my true academic abilities, and would respectfully request that the admissions committee look to (my undergraduate performance in one of my nation’s most competitive colleges / my frequent promotions at a top-tier firm / whatever other proof that you’re just as smart as others ) as a more accurate reflection of my academic capabilities."

For gaps in work experience, don't be afraid to be honest -- AdComs realize that the economy has been rough for several years. Explain the situation in a brief, matter-of-fact way. If you were laid-off, try to provide any evidence that "it wasn't just you". Also try to focus on the positive things you did with / learned from this time:

"Having worked so hard at Merrill Stanley for two years, I was devastated when 80% of my department (including all Analysts) were laid off. I used this time to re-evaluate what I wanted, and realized that I [did / did not] want to continue in banking. With this time, I learned how to seriously network for the first time. I also completed a yoga teacher certification, which has helped me tremendously with managing stress in subsequent years. While the lay-off wasn't fun, I learned a lot from it, which I think have made me a better person and leader."

Statement 1 (After some irrelevant verbiage about various career paths, it then goes on to ask): As you contemplate your post-MBA aspirations, walk us through your career journey thus far and share two post-MBA career paths you aim to explore during the Vanderbilt MBA program. Please articulate what in your career journey has led to your decision to pursue an MBA, and touch on skills you've developed that will help you achieve your post-MBA career plans. (400 words)
In a hurry? How is this '"Statement of Purpose" different from all other "Statements of Purpose"?
You *WILL* keep the SAME long-term hook here: the "hook" is now even MORE important, since you need to describe 2 different paths that might still lead to the same destination. You also MUST EXPLAIN TO THEM how your prior experiences / skills will be useful, even if indirectly, to get that job.
Note that the long-term hook does NOT need to be a specific job; rather, it can be the long-term IMPACT you want to make, e.g.:
"Ever since my father lost his life savings to Bernie Madoff's investment scheme, I vowed to one day open an investment firm that is focused on complete and utter transparency."
Now, in order to figure out what 2 potential paths you could talk about , ask yourself, what are 2 MAIN skills / experiences you'd need to achieve this (let's assume that the person writing this has used the Career Vision module and thus is only saying this because they ALREADY have SOME investing professional experience")? 
The big things you need to run an investment firm are: 
- Thing you need #1: Umm... how to invest? 
- Thing you need #2: How to run a small business (e.g. employees) and perhaps within that, how to convince people to give you their money! (marketing!) 
So, what are 2 different short-term / post-MBA paths that could get you there? 
- Plan A: Working for a big fancy investment firm to learn the best practices in how to invest (the first thing you need above). It will also give you the cache to attract clients to your own fund in the future (the 2nd thing you need) 
- Plan B: Work for a small, boutique investment firm... maybe one that specializes in an area you'd like to focus on (e.g. fixed income)... this would perhaps give you more specialized, deeper experience in one aspect of investing (Thing you need #1)... but would ALSO let you see first-hand how a small firm is run -- how does it recruit, manage, and retain employees? (Thing you need #2)... plus, at a smaller firm, you'll probably get direct client-facing experience sooner, which will help make you more convincing when winning over clients in the future (the 2nd part of thing #2 that you need)
