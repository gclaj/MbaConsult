This is where all of the work you did in the Career Vision module pays off!

This is a very standard "Career Vision / Why MBA? / Why our school?" question, except that now Duke has SUPER-SMUSHED it into one teeeeny 100-word essay!
Here's what you will do: 
1)  Start off with a super-diluted version of the long-term "hook".
-YES I KNOW THAT THEY DON'T ASK FOR IT, but hear me out -- it's kind of weird to say "My short term goal is to work in marketing for a global pharmaceutical company" or  "After business school, I want to work as a product manager for a money-mangement startup!" by itself -- after all, how can I possibly ascertain if the short-term goal makes any sense at all if I have no idea where you want to go in the long term? 
- Given word contraints, this will probbaly end up looking something like: 
"As someone who wants to one day help people in developing markets get access to life-saving drugs, immediately after business school I'll pursue a job in a commercial rotational development program at a pharmaceutical company like Eli Lily, or, alternately, as a marketing manager for a generic drug manufacturer." (the Plan B)
"My father lost his job when I was seven; it turned out he had no money saved, despite a relatively stable income as a contractor. Given that I want to help give middle-class people better access to good savings advice, after business school I want to learn more about how technology is helping people manage their money, either as a product manager or a  marketing manager at a company like SoFi." (the Plan B) 
- DO NOT use the words "long-term goal" in the essay -- but do reference it quickly as more of the "here is the positive impact I hope to make in my life" 
- Note that you can elaborate more on the story behind the long term hook in your "25 Things", if you need to slice of words here to stay under the limit.
2) THEN, if it's not obvious and if you have room, explain WHY the Plan A and the Plan B goals help lead you to your long-term desintation -- this will convey your thoughtfulness about this vs. "Welp! Maybe consulting? Maybe banking? I dunno!" 
"The first path would help introduce me to commercialization strategies for drugs in the developing world; the other would focus more on managing the drug discovery process, but either path can help me make the sort of impact I'd like to make in the world." 
"The product manager role would enhance my current software knowlege by teaching me how to account for customer needs early on in product design; the other role would help me learn how to communicate with and persuade consumers to take their financial health more seriously -- either of these skills will help me one day start a money-management app for contractors and construction workers."
3) Then to address the "why is this the right next step for you?" piece, it may help to establish at least ONE piece of your background that is relevant (e.g. makes you an appealing hire) for that short-term goal... but that ALSO doesn't make you look SO accomplished that you don't need the MBA at all. (note: this will not be necessary if you are staying in a similar field; however, if you're a career-switcher, you might need to sell me on your ability to make that change):
"I've already had some exposure to these issues as a healthcare consultant, but to advance to what I want to do next, I'd need coursework like  Duke's HSM program, and electives like 'Elective Name Goes Here'." 
"To transition from a technical to a business role, I need the strong business background, plus access to the , that Fuqua provides."
4) If needed, if you are making a career change, you may also want to cite your "homework" here that you've done -- talking to people who have told you that this is a feasible change. 
"Fortunately, I've spoken with our SVP of Global Marketing, and she said that with an MBA, I'd be a great fit for either role." 
"After speaking with several of my engineering classmates who have used the MBA to make a similar switch, I'm confident that Fuqua will help me reach my goals."

First Required Essay: The 'Team Fuqua' spirit and community is one of the things that sets the MBA experience apart, and it is a concept that extends beyond the student body to include faculty, staff, and administration. Please share with us “25 Random Things” about you.
The Admissions Committee wants to get to know YOU - beyond the professional and academic achievements listed in your resume and transcript. Share with us important life experiences, your hobbies, achievements, fun facts, or anything that helps us understand what makes you who you are.
Please do not copy the essay question in the document you upload with your application, and limit your response to 2 pages (750 words maximum). Present your response in list form, numbered 1 to 25. Some points may be brief, while others may be longer.
A note on word counts: you do not need to include the listed numbers 1 – 25 in your total word count. Some word processing programs, like MS Word, will count numbers as “words,” but you may exclude the numbers from your final count. The application will allow you to upload your full essay even if MS Word indicates a slightly higher word count.



In a hurry?
EACH of the 25 things should never simply list “THIS IS THE THING ABOUT ME", but should aim to have a little bit of a story behind it. So instead of “For the past two years, I’ve coached my little brother’s soccer team", it should instead include more “story", such as: “For the past two years, I’ve coached my little brother’s soccer team. At first I felt uncomfortable reprimanding him in public, but soon I learned how to separate being his ‘coach’ from being his ‘sister’."
Aim to have about ⅓ of your 25 things being something “fun" / interesting about you (“At any given time, there are 4 different types of brie cheese in my fridge!"), ⅓ being about work -- but NOT a copy of your resume bullets, but rather some detail behind the bullet, why certain accomplishments make you proud, or some back story I would have never guessed (“During the most important product launch of my career, a mouse chewed through the power-cord in the server room, so I had to scramble at 2 am to find the broken cord and tape it together!"), and then ⅓ being related to any extra-curriculars / volunteering / hobbies -- again, not just listing what you do, but “the story behind it". If you don’t have a lot of community stuff to talk about, instead put examples that show you demonstrating those traits, like mentoring / empathy (could be mentoring a younger person at work, for example).
You could group the 25 things into categories, but I think it could be more fun to sprinkle work / non-work examples, just to keep the pace up.
I also advocate STARTING with SOMETHING fun / personal: hook their attention up-front with the sort of person you are before you start digging into the work examples. I also advocate ENDING with something fun / personal -- go out on a high note!
NO MATTER WHAT -- do NOT BRAG in any of yor 25 things -- both their admissions committee and the Dean have said that this is an instant turn-off. So resist the temptation to talk about awards won, etc., just for the sake of trying to mention them / show-off. That will backfire!

Overview of the 25 things -- what are they looking for?
They see this as a way to see if your personality is a "fit" for Team Fuqua -- this is why bragging ("I won an amazing award!") is the WRONG thing to do. 
You can think of these things as falling into combinations of the buckets of:
Past, present, future
Personal, Community, Professional
Therefore, your list can be any of the combinations of the above (e.g.  "past community", "present personal", "future personal (a personal goal)", present professional, etc.  The only combination that doesn't go here is "future professional", since those plans are already outlined in the career essays. 
When in doubt, you can also ask yourself what examples you have from your life that show you demonstrating the "6 paired principles" and/or "EQ and DQ".

Simply re-worded bullet points from the resume

So then, what can you talk about?

Something you learned / how you grew from a particular work experience
Provide otherwise un-guessable context into something that happened at work 
So for example, the resume might tell me that you led a project... but what the resume won't tell me is that that half of the team got laid-off only two weeks in and you had to scramble to keep team morale up. THAT kind of background info / context could make for a good "thing" for your list.

Worried about sounding "braggy" about an accomplishment, but you still want to mention the accomplishment? Use phrases like "honored", "humbled", "it meant so much, because". So instead of: "I was twice awarded the 'best employee' award and given a handsome bonus", it should be more like: "I was so honored when my dedication to nurturing my team was recognized twice via the 'best employee' award."
If you come from an over-represented group (finance or engineering, for example) – try to pick something that flies in the face of the negative stereotype you have (e.g. that finance people are heartless / humorless; that engineers are just rule-following robots).
Also, if you have some sort of “origin story" for your career vision, you can cite that. For instance, I once had a client who worked in the business side of entertainment – his “fun fact" was that he did drama summer camps as a kid. The reason this answer “worked" was not just because it was a fun fact, but also because it helped “prove" that he did indeed have a life-long commitment to the entertainment industry. So – if you work in environmental stuff, can you tell a story about being in nature? If you work in the automotive industry, can you share a fun story about driving a car on the Autobahn?

Some additional thoughts on the 6 principles:
A great way to approach some of these facts is to refer to Team Fuqua’s Principles. In the past, they've had an ENTIRE ESSAY devoted to these six principles, so we know that they’re important! The trick is to list something you have already done that exhibits one of the principles! Try to include all 6:
Authentic Engagement: We care and we take action. We each make a difference to Team Fuqua by being ourselves and engaging in and supporting activities about which we are passionate.
So YOUR examples could include “origin stories" for work/community service things you do, to prove that you are authentically passionate about this industry / cause.
Supportive Ambition: We support each other to achieve great things, because your success is my success. The success of each individual member of Team Fuqua makes the whole of Team Fuqua better.
ANY of your stories about mentoring someone, helping a co-worker, etc. especially helping someone else achieve their ambition, will be perfect here. They want to see that you’ll be the sort of alum who hires other Fuqua students 10+ years from now :)
Collective Diversity: We embrace all of our classmates because our individuality is better and stronger together.
Any story with your “unique" brand trait could go here. If you’re from an over-represented group, you can list something that shows that you have a broader perspective. Professionally, this could be pointing out a unique way you do things (“I’m the only Analyst on my team who volunteers to research aluminum companies; most of my peers would rather research something ‘sexier’ like internet companies, but my dad used to work in an aluminum plant, so that industry means a lot to me.")
You could also use examples from your personal life to show that you have a broader perspective on life. For example, maybe you have a relative who has a learning disability, or who is struggling with a health issue, or who is destitute. (recently, a graduation day speaker at Princeton revealed that her uncle had died from AIDS in the ‘80s, and her family didn’t tell her until she was an adult because they were ashamed of him. This helps show that she empathizes with the gay community, even though at face value she appeared to be just another person with zero “diversity")
Impactful Stewardship: We are leaders who focus on solutions to improve our communities both now and in the future. We aren’t satisfied with just maintaining the status quo.
This is basically saying, "You need to be a leader, duh". :) By which we mean people who take iniative and action to fix things. Note that I think you can "prove" this via the resume / recommendations too.
Loyal Community: We are a family who looks out for each other. Team Fuqua supports you when you need it the most.
Basically the same as “Supportive Ambition": “WE REALLY MEAN THAT STUFF ABOUT TEAM-WORK, EMPATHY, AND COACHING OTHERS, GUYS."
Uncompromising Integrity: We internalize and live the honor code in the classroom and beyond. We conduct ourselves with integrity within Fuqua, within Duke, and within all communities of which we are a part.
If any of your stories is an “ethical dilemma" story, it could be good to include, to support this point. Otherwise, don’t sweat it. “Integrity" is one of those weird ones to “prove". It’s like “being cool": as soon as you say: “I’m SO cool!" you immediately look like you’re not :) Similarly, I feel like saying: “I have SO much integrity!" feels icky. Better to “show" this indirectly by framing it as a “challenge you faced at work (that happened to have an ethical dilemma involved) vs. coming out and sounding lame with: “I’ve always prided myself on my integrity".

 Where will others drop the ball on this essay?
They won’t tie in their future contribution to Duke in with SOMETHING concrete in their past.
They’ll say stuff that they think the AdComs want to hear, like: “I can’t wait to participate in Habitat for Humanity volunteer events, both as part of pre-term and during my entire time at Fuqua…." But -- if this comes from someone who has NEVER before volunteered for anything, saying something like this won’t only sound hollow, it will back-fire if it looks like you’re trying portray yourself as something you aren’t
Given that, a good place to start on this essay is to think about:
Volunteering / Campus / Community things you’ve done IN THE PAST and
Which of your specific skills you can bring to a campus activity
Ie, if you’re making a career switch from [marketing in retail] to [marketing in biotech] and therefore plan to join the Healthcare Club… can you bring your marketing background to help the club publicize its events? If you’re a techie, can you help the club with its website?
BONUS POINTS in this essay will come from not only thoughtfully presenting a (realistic!) sense of your contribution, but also see if you can proactively come up with a suggestion for these clubs RIGHT NOW:
Google the name of the club / conference
Take a look at their events / agenda / speakers
Sometimes they’re even mentioned in an article or blog post
Think about a way you might help them improve, or in the absence of that (their club websites seem a little thin on details?) a respectful suggestion to make, or something you’d advocate that they do.

[The advice for the optional essay is basically the same for all schools, so if you've read this for another school, it will be the same here!]
First of all, if you did have a sort of life-changing event that led to poor academics / periods of unemployment, consider weaving it into a main essay story, to address it head-on. If you do that, then you can potentially leave the optional essay blank. However, many schools have "Career Vision / Why MBA?" types of essays only, so in that case, there's a strong chance you'll need to write an optional essay too.
Keep all optional essays:
Brief
Factual
Not whiny ("poor me / boo hoo!")
Not too apologetic ("I totally suck! You'll probably reject me!")
YES: MATURE. If you made a mistake, own up to it and what you learned from it.
For not using your boss as your recommender: Just quickly explain why, and ALSO why this other person is also qualified to speak about you.
"My current boss does not support my plans for business school, so instead I'm submitting a recommendation from another VP in my department, with whom I have worked very closely for the past two years. She has seen me organize projects, work with others, and sell senior management on new ideas, so I feel that she can provide a strong perspective on my leadership style."
For poor grades:There are all sorts of ways to phrase it, but just state the reason for the bad grades in a matter-of-fact way, followed by the other evidence (of either academic ability, work-ethic, or both) that you have. See if you can stick in (SUBTLY!) a positive personality trait / or "good lesson learned" in there too, such as not giving up:
"Coming from an under-funded public high school with no AP science/math classes (very few science classes, period), adapting to the engineering curriculum at Cal was a bumpy ride, to say the least. While it was tempting to drop out and major in economics instead, I persevered and ultimately completed the BSE degree. I'm glad I stuck with it, since it taught me that I could tackle any challenge. I'd respectfully ask that the Admissions Committee look at my GMAT score and my strong dedication to my job as proof that I will be able to thrive in the MBA academic environment. "
For gaps in work experience, don't be afraid to be honest -- AdComs realize that the economy has been rough for several years. Explain the situation in a brief, matter-of-fact way. If you were laid-off, try to provide any evidence that "it wasn't just you". Also try to focus on the positive things you did with / learned from this time:
"Having worked so hard at Merrill Stanley for two years, I was devastated when 80% of my department (including all Analysts) were laid off. I used this time to re-evaluate what I wanted, and realized that I [did / did not] want to continue in banking. With this time, I learned how to seriously network for the first time. I also completed a yoga teacher certification, which has helped me tremendously with managing stress in subsequent years. While the lay-off wasn't fun, I learned a lot from it, which I think have made me a better person and leader."
