Essay 2: Taking into consideration your background – personal, professional, and/or academic – how do you plan to add meaningful value to the Wharton community? (350 words)

Essay 2 Response: 
At Wharton, I’ll show up as a bridge-builder and friend: someone who creates moments of connection, belonging, and growth across cultures and communities. 

I grew up with my Puerto Rican grandfather’s stories of sacrifice and resilience. Service defined both my childhood while delivering meals to struggling families in Inner-City Baltimore, and my military career, where on several occasions I stood in the open door of a C-130 preparing to lead soldiers into a parachute assault. Teaching francophone populations in both the U.S. and Africa how to budget, save, and launch microenterprises made me a more empathetic, globally minded leader. 

These experiences uniquely position me to create a cross-campus partnership with the Philadelphia African Family Health Organization. Working with peers from the Social Impact Club and Africa Business Forum, I’ll design language-specific personal budgeting and entrepreneurial workshops, similar to those I’ve previously taught in communities around the world. This project will enable students from across Wharton and Lauder to help create meaningful cross-cultural connections, becoming cognizant global citizens grounded in service.  

Just as importantly, I’ll help create spaces where students feel seen and welcomed. I’ll host cultural dinners, invite classmates to Vermont ski weekends, and make informal dialogue spaces where veterans, international students, and affinity groups can genuinely listen to one another. While living abroad in Europe and Africa, I’ve seen how even small gestures can transform isolation into a sense of belonging. I want every classmate, regardless of their background, to feel valued and important while at Wharton.

While in the military, I’ve seen strong leaders create teams and establish systems that cultivate potential and unlock success. While at Wharton, I aim to do the same by fostering cultural fluency and community partnerships, demonstrating to Wharton students the potential they have as forces for good in the world. Helping cultivate future business leaders who enable enterprises, develop markets, and serve underserved populations, through business, is the impact I hope to have at Wharton.



Lauder Essay 1: Discuss why you are applying to the Lauder Institute, detailing specific reasons, experiences, and background for choosing your Program of Concentration, as well as your interest in further study in that Program. Applicants to Programs of Concentration involving language study (Africa-French) should also discuss what they hope to gain from advanced language and cultural education, as well as describe how they have learned the language they will study (e.g. undergraduate courses, undergraduate major, etc.). Finally, please describe how you see yourself benefiting from, and contributing to, the Lauder community.   (500 words)

Lauder Essay 1 Response:
While living in Cameroon and the Republic of the Congo, I watched Chinese workers erect massive infrastructure projects in exchange for hundreds of freight trucks carrying valuable African minerals and resources to Chinese ports. Later, I saw those same buildings collapse during Africa’s monsoon season due to hasty construction and unethical building standards. Witnessing China’s Belt and Road Initiative firsthand allowed me to see the hidden consequences that affect developing economies when interacting with world powers. 

My experiences have taught me that Sub-Saharan Africa needs allies with the proper perspective to help African economies tap into their potential. I envision becoming that ally, helping lead operational transformation within African-based businesses. 

To become this ally, I need to develop deeper cultural fluency, establish a network of globally minded peers, and gain a sophisticated understanding of the region's business and policy landscape. The Wharton-Lauder MBA/MA is the only program that offers this holistic preparation. 

Through the Africa-French concentration, I’ll enhance my understanding of francophone cultural contexts while refining my French to a professional level. I learned French through immersion while serving as an international volunteer, but I never had the opportunity to formally study the language. Studying French with Marc Papé at Lauder will expose me to essential grammar rules and cultural nuances, helping me prepare to interact with French African business leaders. 

By traveling back to Africa with Lauder’s Summer Immersion Program and International Ventures, I’ll gain on-the-ground exposure to the cultural and policy challenges shaping Sub-Saharan African markets. 

While I am eager to learn more about the contextual complexities surrounding African economies, I am equally excited to engage with the community found at Lauder. It has been a personal struggle to find those who share my passion for Africa and belief in its global potential. Lauder will provide me with a network that both recognizes the value of Sub-Saharan Africa and is committed to helping the region thrive. 

Beyond academics and community, Lauder offers an environment where I can showcase African potential through the Africa Futures Symposium. While in Africa, I observed the construction of virtual campus learning environments sponsored by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (LDS). These unique initiatives have created stable pathways for African communities to access higher education, creating stability in volatile areas. To demonstrate how innovative Western partnerships can expand untapped potential, I will organize a panel featuring Clark Gilbert and Gérald Caussé, the LDS authorities overseeing and resourcing African education programs, and Lauder faculty member Kanyinsola Arigi, where the link between education, regional stability, and economic development will be explored. This panel will offer Lauder classmates firsthand insights into ethical international partnerships, equipping future leaders with frameworks to navigate complex cross-border investments. 

My military and grassroots experiences in Francophone Africa make me a unique Lauder candidate. However, I can’t reach my goals with only my experiences. What I lack, I will find at Wharton-Lauder: more profound regional knowledge, meaningful relationships, and the tools to bridge theory and practice, becoming the ally Africa deserves.




Lauder Essay 2: Imagine you’re in a classroom and a fellow student makes a statement you find offensive or problematic. How would you respond? You may use a real experience you’ve had or outline how you’d approach the situation. What principles would guide you, and what would you hope the outcome would be? (200 words)

Lauder Essay 2 Response:
I’ve found that the most valuable classrooms are often the most uncomfortable. In academia, ideas must be freely shared, without fear of retribution. It is equally essential to remember that while a person’s beliefs may influence their character, they do not define the individual. Sharing ideas, especially those in dissent, fosters both personal and community growth.

In my final year at West Point, following the politically charged era of the COVID-19 pandemic, I participated in a discussion-based forum where I shared several personal anecdotes that helped shape my worldview. Months later, I learned that my shared perspective had offended others. Instead of sharing their discomfort, they remained silent. That day, I missed an opportunity to grow. 

At Lauder, when faced with views I find offensive, I’ll respond by challenging the idea, not the person, and sharing my perspective in the hope that the ensuing discussion offers valuable insights that deepen our understanding and broaden our perspectives. Through thoughtful, diverse discussions, my Lauder classmates and I can expand our collective worldviews and grow into more conscientious global citizens.
