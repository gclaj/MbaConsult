Essay 1: The 'Team Fuqua' spirit and community is one of the things that sets the MBA experience apart, and it is a concept that extends beyond the student body to include faculty, staff, and administration. Please share with us “25 Random Things” about you.

Essay 1 Response: 

I grew up climbing ancient ruins in Ephesus and Assos in Turkey, fulfilling my childhood dreams of becoming Indiana Jones. Those early adventures sparked my lifelong curiosity about history and exploration.
Born in Turkey, I've spent a quarter of my life abroad, discovering early on that people everywhere have far more in common than differences. It's a lesson I carry into every interaction today.
My mother is Puerto Rican, and growing up, my grandfather's stories of leaving Ponce to join the Army and chase the American Dream inspired me deeply. Years later, I followed in his footsteps by joining the exact unit he served in: the 82nd Airborne Division.
I've lived in ten states, but Vermont, where my family finally settled, taught me the true meaning of home and still is my refuge.
After two years at West Point, I made the challenging decision to resign and serve a two-year mission for The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints in sub-Saharan Africa. The lessons in empathy and humility I gained there reshaped my perspective forever.
As a missionary in Ghana, Cameroon, and Congo, I formed friendships and community ties that taught me the profound value of human connection across cultures.
I spent so much time in Cameroon that due to a paperwork error, I accidentally became a legal resident until 2035! I cherish the clerical error as a humorous token of my deep-rooted connections there.
In Congo, I mastered cooking Fou Fou, a labor-intensive dish that taught me patience, hospitality, and the joy found in sharing meals and stories. 
While at West Point, a four-week Hebrew immersion program in Jerusalem deepened my appreciation for the intersection of history, faith, and culture. 
I first bonded with my wife during late-night American History study sessions at West Point, a friendship that quickly became our lifelong partnership.
The coolest date I've ever planned was a static-line parachute jump with my wife at the 82nd Airborne Division - it’s still unbeatable.
Leading one of just thirteen airborne mortar platoons in the U.S. Army taught me that leadership is more about listening than commanding. I learned this after a young soldier’s feedback improved our team's communication plan significantly.
Completing over 20 static-line parachute jumps, including five in one intense week at Airborne School, taught me enduring lessons about managing fear and trusting teammates.
When my roommate at West Point convinced me to enter a Norwegian Ruck March, we spontaneously ran the entire 18.75 miles wearing 30-lb packs instead of marching. My shoulders have never forgiven me, but the camaraderie was worth every step.
Halfway through Ranger School, I broke my ribs rappelling down a mountain, forcing me to rely on teammates and teaching me firsthand about resilience, humility, and grit.
During a short break between Ranger School phases, I read 12 books in one week: my mental survival strategy for navigating the toughest training of my life.
I participated in the final legendary West Point Plebe Pillow Fight of 2019, leaving with a concussion, an unexpected CNN headline, and an unforgettable story.

When I was cut from the Army Lacrosse team freshman year after a grueling preseason, I stayed on as team manager for two years because I couldn't bear to leave the brotherhood we had cultivated. Supporting them from the sidelines gave me a new way to lead. 
Serving as a West Point Company Commander taught me that real leadership isn't about giving orders, but about cultivating trust and mentorship. I’ll never forget when a former cadet called years later to say my advice helped him through Ranger School.
My favorite authors are Victor Hugo and Alexandre Dumas: Les Misérables taught me mercy and forgiveness; The Count of Monte Cristo, the futility of revenge and hate.
Despite my love for classic literature, I'm a die-hard Brandon Sanderson fan. I proudly collect his leatherbound editions and hunt down signatures at book signings whenever I can.
"Let It Go" is the undefeated soundtrack for my heavy lifts. It reminds me that true strength can come from unlikely places.
I can recite every hymn in the Latter-Day Saint hymnbook by heart. During trainings, those songs became my anchor and were quiet reminders of home and hope.
I have a collection of 18 wooden animal figurines from across Africa, including a giraffe from Kenya and an elephant from Congo, each symbolizing unforgettable friendships and places that shaped who I am today.

Essay 2: Fuqua prides itself on cultivating a culture of engagement. Our students enjoy a wide range of student-led organizations that provide opportunities for leadership development and personal fulfillment, as well as an outlet for contributing to society. Our student-led government, clubs, centers, and events are an integral part of the student culture and to the development of leaders. Based on your understanding of the Fuqua culture, what are 3 ways you expect to contribute at Fuqua? (500 words)

Essay 2 Response: 
In the pouring rain, tucked away in north Georgia’s mountains, I clung to a rope, my shattered ribs pulsing with every breath. It was Day 2 of Ranger School’s Mountain Phase, and I had just slammed into the cliff face during a 50-foot descent. The following three weeks, low on sleep and food, became a masterclass in humility, sacrifice, and endurance. My Ranger buddies supported me when I needed them most, carrying my load when I couldn’t and backing me as I led missions. Their selflessness forged bonds of mutual trust and resilience.
At Fuqua, I will recreate this spirit as an Expedition Chair within the BOLD Club. Leveraging my Ranger School and Army experiences, I will organize an annual Mount Whitney trek that blends intense physical challenges with professional development, including five days on the trail followed by immersive company visits in Silicon Valley. This shared journey will cultivate deep friendships and leadership capabilities, vital both in boardrooms and on mountain trails.
Years before Ranger School, I sat with Jacques, a Cameroonian electrical engineer living outside Yaoundé. All he needed to start a poultry business and gain financial freedom for his family were a few rolls of chicken wire, yet without accessible funding, his dream stalled. Witnessing Jacques' unmet potential profoundly impacted me, illuminating the fragile nature of opportunity, and its transformative power if unlocked.
To address such gaps, at Fuqua I will partner closely with the CASE Center faculty to establish a GATE course to Sub-Saharan Africa. Drawing on extensive personal and professional relationships developed during my years volunteering in Cameroon and the Republic of the Congo, I will coordinate impactful site visits with vetted entrepreneurs and facilitate in-depth sessions exploring ethical investment and capital allocation in emerging markets. Additionally, I will leverage my experiences to contribute actively as a CASE i3 Fellow, developing strategies to direct impact investment toward promising African ventures, ultimately empowering entrepreneurs like Jacques. My past experiences coordinating humanitarian logistics give me practical familiarity with managing international engagements. 
Finally, through the Duke Armed Forces Association (DAFA), I will champion inclusive leadership and veteran integration as the Social & Recruiting Chair. Early in my Army career, I experienced firsthand how overlooked potential can flourish when someone invests belief and support. One morning, I handed a race bib to Sergeant Lauren Burd when others doubted her place on our unit’s team. She defied expectations and later proudly represented our unit at the 150th D-Day Anniversary in Normandy. Inspired by experiences like this, I will expand DAFA’s outreach to prospective veteran applicants, organize mentorship pairings between incoming veterans and second-year mentors, and host the club's “Vet Talks”, fostering greater understanding within Fuqua’s community by promoting leadership lessons from diverse military experiences.
Each of these contributions is rooted deeply in my experiences and convictions: fostering camaraderie through shared challenge, unlocking potential through thoughtful investment, and nurturing inclusive communities by elevating diverse voices. This approach aligns with my core values and encapsulates what “Team Fuqua” means to me.

Optional Essay Response: 
During my early years at West Point, I struggled with academic immaturity while also managing serious family challenges. My sister was in and out of drug rehabilitation programs after running away from home, which placed a heavy emotional burden on me during a formative period of my education. While I take full responsibility for my academic performance, these circumstances contributed to my 2.77 GPA.

That said, my academic trajectory improved significantly after I returned from a two-year mission in sub-Saharan Africa. I maintained a GPA above 3.0 for the remainder of my time at West Point, made the Dean’s List in my final semesters, and was honored with the Superintendent’s Award for Academic Achievement. More recently, I completed Harvard Business School’s Credential of Readiness (CORe) program and graduated with honors, demonstrating my ability to excel in rigorous, graduate-level coursework.

Regarding my GRE score (157 Verbal / 161 Quant), I understand it may appear modest relative to Fuqua’s average. However, I would respectfully ask the committee to consider it in the context of my quantitative undergraduate curriculum in engineering management, my strong finish at West Point, and my distinction in the HBS CORe program. I believe these collectively reflect my readiness for the MBA classroom.

I would also like to address my choice of recommenders. As an Army officer, requesting a recommendation from my current supervisor at the 101st Airborne Division could adversely affect assignment opportunities. For this reason, I have chosen former supervisors who are highly familiar with my leadership capabilities and impact. These individuals can speak to my strengths in managing diverse teams, driving cross-functional outcomes, and leading through ambiguity.

Finally, I would like to clarify two gaps in my professional timeline. From June 2021 to September 2022, I was participating in the U.S. Army’s Infantry Basic Officer Leader Course, Airborne School, and Ranger School, full-time, intensive training programs required for operational leadership. From October 2024 to May 2025, I attended the Maneuver Captains Career Course, where I was again earned a spot on the Commandant’s List. These gaps are not employment interruptions but rather career milestones in Army officer development.

I share this context not to make excuses, but to offer a fuller picture of my journey. These experiences have shaped my resilience, discipline, and commitment to growth, qualities I will carry with me to Fuqua and beyond.
