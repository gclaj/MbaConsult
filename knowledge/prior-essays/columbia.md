Essay 1: Through your resume and recommendations, we have a clear sense of your professional path to date. What are your career goals over the next three to five years and what is your long-term dream job? (Maximum 500 Words)

Essay 1 Response: 
As rain seeped into his cinderblock home outside Yaoundé, Cameroon, Jacques, an aspiring entrepreneur, and I calculated the cost of starting a poultry business. It became clear that his limited access to capital and operational leadership would hinder his entrepreneurial journey. Leaving, I realized Jacques had the drive to succeed, but not the opportunity to act on it. That day, I became determined to bridge that gap by becoming a leader who could build systems and optimize capital to expand opportunities in developing economies.

To accomplish this, over the next three to five years, I aim to join a strategy consulting firm like Bain or BCG, focusing on operational value creation work for PE portfolio companies. While leading a strategic military support unit, I advised senior leaders on the deployment and use of strategic assets vital to the success of intense parachute assaults. During these missions, I learned to optimize mission strategy and operational requirements to deliver a measurable impact. These experiences have inspired me to continue optimizing strategy outside the Army as a strategy consultant. But, to do so effectively, I need to pair my military experiences with a foundation in financial modeling, working capital management, and market strategies. CBS’s Core Curriculum will build the financial foundation I need to complement my operational experience. By pairing core courses like Foundations of Valuation with Bruno Pellegrino and electives like Driving Strategic Impact with Marco Viola Kim, I’ll build vital financial skills that'll directly prepare me to intern and pivot into strategy consulting after graduating from CBS.   

My long-term dream is to leverage my experience in strategy consulting to transition into an operations role at an African-focused private equity (PE) firm, such as Helios, where I will lead operational transformation across Sub-Saharan Africa. While leading high-stakes AI-integration projects in several military units, I worked with disjointed stakeholders, similar to operational value creation within PE portfolio firms. This exposure to execution complexity, combined with experiences mentoring underserved populations in Sub-Saharan Africa, sparked my interest in post-investment transformation. However, to drive transformation in frontier markets, I’ll need to pair operational acumen with a deep understanding of capital allocation and regional market strategy. I’ll bridge this gap by studying at CBS’s Chazen Institute during my second year at CBS, taking Stephen Meier’s Global Immersion Tech in Africa courses, which focus on both Kenya and Ghana, providing valuable insights into capital deployment strategies and African markets. Chazen’s Africa Business Initiative, along with CBS’s NYC location, will provide the platform and environment for me to engage with others passionate about African markets while providing access to African-focused funds headquartered in the City. 

Combining my life experiences with CBS’s opportunities will enable me to achieve my future. In speaking with both Manuel Prieto (CBS’25) and Evan Gunn (CBS’26), I’ve come to see that CBS is not just a way to pivot into consulting, but as a transformational program capable of shaping me into the leader Jacques needed on that rainy day in Cameroon.   


Essay 2: Please share a specific example of how you made a team more collaborative, more inclusive or fostered a greater sense of community within an organization. (Maximum 250 words)

Essay 2 Response: 
“Sir, if she runs, we won’t win.” It was the morning of the “All-American 10-Miler”, a prestigious race with 60+ teams from Fort Bragg, and my assistant coach wanted to cut Lauren, the only woman on our team. 

As we spoke, my assistant reasoned that neither of us knew if Lauren could perform against male-dominated teams, reminding me our commander had stressed the importance of winning this high-profile event. As we debated, I felt drawn to his reasoning and the intense pressure we felt to win. Suddenly, I realized I was weighing a perceived advantage over principled leadership. Lauren had outperformed competitors during our tryouts, and I’d seen women exceed expectations in the toughest environments, including U.S. Army Ranger School. If I caved, I’d be sending the wrong message to the rest of the team and myself: that I cared more about possible success than proven potential. 

I stopped the conversation and handed Lauren a bib. 

We didn’t win, but Lauren placed first among all female competitors, earning her a spot at the 80th anniversary of D-Day at Normandy. After the race, my assistant apologized, highlighting that my values-driven decision had reshaped the definition of success for our team’s culture.  

That experience taught me that building inclusive teams isn’t about making popular decisions, but about creating an environment where talent is trusted and potential is developed. At CBS, I’ll bring this leadership philosophy with me as I empower others to lead and grow, even when it requires difficult, values-driven choices.

This project will allow me to share my passion for African development with classmates while generating impact. I aim to co-create with CBS peers and alumni throughout my career. 

These experiences with my classmates will define my transition from the military, rooting me in service to communities in NYC and development in Africa.
