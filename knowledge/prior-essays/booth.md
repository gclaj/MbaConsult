Essay 1: How will the Booth MBA help you achieve your immediate and long-term post-MBA career goals? (250 word minimum)

Essay 1 Response: 
Chicken wire. That’s what stood between Jacques, an out-of-work Cameroonian electrical engineer, and his dream of financial security. Sitting with him in his three-walled cinderblock home outside Yaoundé, we calculated the modest cost of launching a poultry business. All he needed was wire to build a coop. But with no access to capital, no bank nearby, and no investor willing to bet on a village entrepreneur, the wire couldn't be funded and Jacques's dream could never be realized. That moment, and dozens like it during my two years volunteering in Sub-Saharan Africa, reshaped my understanding of freedom.
Two years prior, I had entered West Point believing freedom meant security. But in Africa, I realized true freedom is opportunity: the ability to act on your potential and build something lasting. Returning to West Point, I decided to become a leader capable of creating opportunities for the Jacques of the world. Now, after five years leading in the Army, I’m ready to gain the business skills to support ventures in emerging markets. Booth, with its flexible curriculum, global network, and culture of rigorous inquiry, is where I’ll make that pivot. 
Immediately after attending Booth, I will transition into advising investors at a top consulting firm like Bain or BCG, specializing in emerging market strategy and private equity advisory. There, I’ll apply military-honed leadership to growth strategy, operations, and value creation. Conversations with Booth students Alexandra Davis ('25) and Brandon Bowers (’26) confirmed Booth's unmatched support for veterans pivoting into consulting.
Mid-term, I plan to move into a private equity firm, gaining exposure to the full investment lifecycle, from capital allocation to post-acquisition operational execution. Booth’s Private Equity Lab will directly support this transition by strengthening my ability to underwrite complex deals, support portfolio companies, and lead multidisciplinary teams, preparing me for my long-term goal. 
Long-term, I aspire to back African entrepreneurs in Francophone frontier markets. However, I want to do more than simply deploy capital. My goal is to foster mentorship, operational support, and market access to help build sustainable ventures. I will challenge exploitative investment models that historically drained value from African economies and instead promote partnership, patience, and long-term prosperity. Fluent in French and inspired by my experiences on the ground, I’m especially committed to unlocking capital in Francophone Africa. Through conversations with Rodrigue Djikeuchi ('25), I know that Booth’s flexible curriculum and global network are essential and will help me prepare for each stage of this journey.
At Booth, I will concentrate in Analytic Finance and Entrepreneurship, pairing rigorous financial training with an innovation mindset. Entrepreneurial Finance and Private Equity will equip me to structure and evaluate deals, advise clients on value creation, and prepare for hands-on investing in the future. Commercializing Innovation, taught by Professor Scott Meadow, will hone my ability to assess scalability and market fit, skills vital for consulting engagements now and for evaluating grassroots ventures long-term. I also plan to engage with the Center for Applied AI, where courses will enhance my ability to use data and technology in investment decision-making, particularly in resource-constrained contexts like Francophone Africa. These tools will not only differentiate me as a consultant but position me to identify and scale impactful ventures more effectively. Through LEAD and Interpersonal Dynamics, I will sharpen the interpersonal and coaching skills essential to advising clients, mentoring entrepreneurs, and leading multicultural teams.

Outside the classroom, I look forward to shaping speaker events with Booth Africa that connect public policy and private investment in frontier markets. I will work with the Rustandy Center for Social Sector Innovation to refine ethical investment frameworks and propose programming that supports sustainable entrepreneurship. I also hope to mentor fellow veterans pursuing purpose-driven careers, and through the Private Equity Group, gain hands-on experience with early-stage ventures that build both investing judgment and networks for my long-term goals.
Leadership is not a spectator sport. True leaders don’t wait for opportunity; they create it. Booth’s rigorous environment, global community, and culture of bold inquiry will refine my judgment, sharpen my leadership, and empower me to help entrepreneurs like Jacques transform resource constraints into generational prosperity.




Essay 2: An MBA is as much about personal growth as it is about professional development. In addition to sharing your experience and goals in terms of career, we’d like to learn more about you outside of the office. Use this opportunity to tell us something about who you are. (250-word minimum)

Essay 2 Response: 
Each Sunday in Brazzaville, I sat cross-legged in a humid apartment, flipping through stacks of handwritten reports from the 35 volunteers I supported. Each tracked new participants, local partnerships, and project impact. Some teams thrived; others struggled. As I reviewed each page, sweat clinging to my shirt, I felt a strong desire to help, not for the sake of metrics, but because I saw how deeply my peers cared. Their effort deserved better outcomes.
At first, the data felt scattered. But then something clicked. The numbers reminded me of a shape I hadn’t seen since a West Point geography class: population pyramids.
As a kid, I loved How It’s Made, fascinated by how small parts formed complex systems. That curiosity stuck with me, and in that moment, it reemerged with purpose. I pulled six months of reports and sketched modified population pyramids for each project. The visuals revealed hidden trends and gave me a new way to coach my peers. I began visiting each site with tailored feedback: “Your base is wide, but your follow-through is thin. Let’s work on retention.”
Some peers worried a structured tool might oversimplify inherently human work. Instead of pushing harder, I listened, reframed the graphs as supportive guides, and adapted collaboratively. Slowly, trust grew. Eventually, the model was adopted across eight cities in four countries, increasing regional participation by 41%.
This experience reinforced a lesson that now shapes how I lead: leadership isn't about imposing control, it's about empowering others with systems that respect human complexity and foster real growth.
Later, this understanding deepened when my wife and I were stationed in separate states after graduating West Point. Living alone in a bare room, I felt isolated. Then came an invitation to volunteer at a Latter-Day Saint Temple: a sacred place of worship. I accepted, waking at 4 a.m., driving two hours, and volunteering nearly ten hours every Saturday for eight months.
The work was simple but meaningful. Helping others reconnect with their faith gave my weeks structure and purpose. I learned that service isn’t always about charity; sometimes it's about persistence, presence, and quiet care.

Those same traits surfaced again when I failed the first phase of Ranger School and was recycled into the next cohort. Instead of staying quiet, I chose to volunteer as the new class leader in the cohort, choosing to share my failures to help others avoid the same mistakes. 

I led late-night reviews, coached struggling teammates, and helped foster a culture that prioritized teamwork rather than individuality. When 87% of the group passed, I felt proud not of myself, but of the culture we created together.

Despite failing, I chose not to hide. As in my other experiences, I turned to serve those around me, creating systems that enabled others to succeed and reach their potential. This experience reinforced a lesson I carry forward: leaders who leave lasting impacts often do so by owning their mistakes for the good of the team.

These experiences reflect the kind of collaborative, data-driven leadership I aim to bring to Booth. Programs like LEAD and Booth’s experiential labs will allow me to hone my ability to turn insights into action. Through clubs like BOLD and Giving Something Back, I’ll further refine my leadership and empathy and apply systems insights to create real-world, human-centered impact.  I look forward to bringing this blend of systems thinking and service to classroom discussions, team projects, and the broader Booth and Chicago community.
